Scientific Illustration Design

A publication figure needs more than attractive shapes: its labels, pathways and relationships must communicate the science clearly. BioPlot helps you prepare a scientific illustration brief for a research paper, review, thesis or presentation.

Request a scientific illustrationHow it works

Which scientific figures can be illustrated?

Scientific figure design can cover cell-signaling pathways, molecular interactions, biological mechanisms, conceptual models and experimental workflows. Describe the entities, arrows and relationships your figure must show, and supply the references needed to check terminology and scientific meaning.

  • Cell-signaling and molecular pathway diagrams with clear activation and inhibition links
  • Experimental workflow schematics, study designs and research-process visuals
  • Biological mechanisms, cellular processes and conceptual models with readable labels

What do you need to request a scientific figure?

  1. Describe the mechanism, process or research question the illustration should explain.
  2. Share relevant papers, rough sketches, existing diagrams or a list of essential components.
  3. Specify journal dimensions, label language and any formatting requirements.

When is a custom scientific illustration useful?

A pathway schematic or mechanism diagram can support original research, review articles, theses and scientific presentations. Tell us where the figure will appear so the amount of detail and intended audience are clear before design begins.

What should your scientific figure brief include?

List proteins, cell types, directional links, key labels and scientific references. Include any journal figure instructions and desired output format, so feasibility and delivery requirements can be agreed on before work begins.

Scientific figure design questions

Can I request an illustration without a sketch?
You can start with a written description, references and a list of required elements. The project discussion can establish whether the concept provides enough detail to proceed.
How is a scientific figure different from a graphical abstract?
A scientific figure usually explains a particular pathway, process or method in detail. A graphical abstract condenses the overall message of a paper into a single visual summary.
